Give calls as a time

How commanders think about time in the Castle Battle

September 27, 2026 CommandClock

As overall commander, I’ll write about how I think about time in the Castle Battle. These are ideas you can take home whether or not you use the tool. Examples are from Whiteout Survival (unofficial).

I wrote about what happens from the moment the enemy state is decided until the battle ends in What a commander does before SvS.

First, the terms used in this article

Rally leader
The player who starts a rally. Players joining the rally travel to the rally leader’s city during the rally time.
Chain attack
A single attack (rally) rarely breaks through, and when the enemy’s flag holder in the garrison is strong, you add more rallies to break it. That’s a chain attack. Two players make a chain of 2; three make a chain of 3.
Flag holder
The strongest player in the garrison. The player at the very front is sometimes also called the flag holder, so here we use it to mean the strongest player.
Swap
The attacking alliance hands a captured base to the occupying alliance, timed to the second. Players in the occupying alliance land solo.
Withdraw
During a swap, everyone garrisoned inside the base leaves at the same moment.
Reinforce
Sending troops in between the enemy’s chained landings to strengthen the defense.

1. Every second of delay in a call becomes the enemy’s occupation time

When an enemy starts a rally, act on the assumption that they will break your garrison. The time from being broken until the attacking alliance breaks that enemy becomes the enemy’s occupation time. So when an enemy rallies, start a rally right away so you can break them.

The delay comes from calculating the landing time and rounding the seconds to a clean number. On top of that, each person who receives the call has to do the subtraction “landing time − my march time.”

That delay before the call is, in the end, occupation time we let the enemy gain.

2. Why each player’s moment to press is different

The moment to press is different for each person. Teammates’ cities are scattered across the map, so each one’s march time to the base is different. To land at the same time, farther players have to press earlier, and closer players later.

Player A, whose march takes 3 minutes, presses at 21:00:15. Player D, whose march takes 45 seconds, presses at 21:02:30. They press at different times, but everyone is calculated to land at 21:03:15.

3. Give calls as a time, not “in so many seconds”

With “60 seconds from now,” the delay starts the instant someone hears it. With a time, even someone who sees it late can catch up.

So give calls as a landing time. In chat, post it as four digits: minutes and seconds. “1234” means landing at 12 minutes 34 seconds.

4. Match the landing time to the farthest rally leader

Players joining a rally travel to the rally leader’s city during the rally time. What decides the landing is the march time of the rally leader farthest from Sunfire Castle.

5. If you split chains, split the landing times too

Put seven rally leaders on the same landing, and you get a chain of 7. To split it into a chain of 4 and a chain of 3, split them into squads and give each its own landing time.

6. For withdraw, each player leaves on their own

During a swap, everyone from the attacking alliance in the garrison leaves Sunfire Castle a few seconds before the occupying alliance’s solo marches arrive. That’s the withdraw.

The flag holder, alliance leader, and officers can remove players from the garrison, but removing them one by one won’t make it in a one-second race. Each player in the garrison leaves on their own, at the same moment.

7. Don’t trust device clocks

As long as everyone watches their own phone clock, you’ll be off by a few seconds.

I built this math into a tool. It’s called CommandClock.
