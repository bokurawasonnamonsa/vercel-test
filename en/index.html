THE PROBLEM

Does this sound familiar?

You can't win unless many players arrive at the same moment

Be a few seconds off, and the enemy targets that gap and takes the base from you.

Leaving together on “3, 2, 1” doesn't mean arriving together

Everyone starts from a different spot, so the distance and time to the target are different for each person. Even if you all leave on “3, 2, 1,” you won't arrive together, which makes it hard to line up.

The only ways to sync are chat and everyone's own math

Each person watches a phone clock or an analog clock and works out when to leave, one by one.

The leader carries it all alone

The leader does all the math and all the relaying, and sometimes, by the time the call goes out, the target arrival time has already passed!

WHY TIMING MATTERS

Why line up landing times?

  1. 1

    One attack rarely takes a base.

    To take a base, you have to defeat every troop defending inside. A single attack (rally) rarely breaks through, and when the enemy's flag holder in the garrison is strong, you add more rallies to break it. This is called a “chain attack.”

    *Flag holder: the strongest player in the garrison. The player at the very front is sometimes also called the flag holder, so here we use it to mean the strongest player.

  2. 2

    Gaps between attacks let the defense rebuild.

    If even one of three chained rallies is a few seconds late, the enemy slips troops in during those seconds and shores up the defense. This is “reinforcing.” You can't wear down the garrison, and you can't break through. That's why attackers want to line up landing times and leave no gaps.

  3. 3

    Defenders aim for that gap.

    Send troops timed to land between the enemy's chained landings, and you can refill the defense. Attackers and defenders are fighting over the same few seconds.

  4. 4

    Handing a base to an ally is a race against the clock, too.

    When you hand a captured base to an allied alliance, everyone inside has to leave at the same moment, a few seconds before the ally arrives.

  5. 5

    Those seconds add up to the result.

    The winner is decided by how long each side occupied the base. Every second lost taking it, and every second lost when it's taken back, becomes the difference in occupation time.

HOW IT WORKS

It supports strategies 1–4 by telling everyone the moment to press.

Every one of 1–4 succeeds or fails on whether everyone presses at the agreed time. CommandClock is a support tool to raise that success rate, even a little.

The moment to press is different for each person. Teammates' cities are scattered across the map, so each one's march time to the base is different. To land at the same time, farther players have to press earlier, and closer players later.

When the caller presses the call button, the tool sets the landing time: the current time, plus a buffer before pressing, the rally time, and the longest march time. It picks a time even the farthest player can make, so no one gets left behind.

Then it subtracts each player's march time to work out their moment to press, and shows it on their phone as a countdown. If everyone presses at 0, the landings line up.

Press together on “3, 2, 1”: landings scatter
Press on your countdown: landings line up

Because march times differ, pressing together makes the closest players land first. Press in order from farthest to closest, and everyone lands at the same time.

Supports 1 & 2

Land chain attacks with no gaps

Everyone starting a rally gets the same landing time and their own moment to press. If everyone presses at that moment, the landings line up, shrinking the gap the enemy can reinforce through.

Supports 3

Hit the gaps in the enemy's chain

There are only a few seconds between the enemy's landings. You get a landing time aimed at that gap and the moment to press, so you can send reinforcements timed to the second.

Supports 4

Make handoffs in time, when every second counts

Everyone inside the base gets a countdown to the moment to leave. If the leader pulls people out one by one, it's too late. Each player leaves on their own at the same moment, so the handoff makes it in time (swap and withdraw).

ON THE DAY

Every second lost before the call becomes the enemy's occupation time.

Before the tool

For the five hours of the Castle Battle, I had three accounts open at the same time.

It was the account I kept on standby in the occupying alliance that told me the enemy had started a rally. When they did, I gave the landing time to the attacking alliance's rally leaders over a voice-chat app.

The delay came from calculating the landing time and rounding the seconds to a clean number. That delay before the call is, in the end, occupation time we let the enemy gain. If I had been able to give those rally calls quickly, I think we could easily have gained six seconds.

Everyone who got the call also worked out “landing time − my march time” one by one before pressing. There were mishearings, seconds off, and confusion over who was doing what.

After the tool

What removed that delay was CommandClock's Call now. When the enemy rallies, press the Call now button. That alone gets the call to everyone with no delay. No wasted math and no rounding to clean numbers: I can now give rally calls at the best possible moment. Landing-time calls became concrete, swaps and reinforcing included. The situation became far easier to read, everyone moved precisely, and the precision of our strategy went up enormously.

Since we started using the tool, our opponents have been telling us, “Your coordination is solid. High-quality landings and swaps!” We hear it from many players in the same opposing state, and from players in other states too, almost every time.

WHY IT EXISTS

A tool built with real effort by a strategist new to strategy games, to raise the server's win rate.

The biggest reason I built this tool:
we fought for five hours and lost by six seconds.

We fought the full five hours and lost by six seconds. The Castle Battle, where servers fight over a base, lasts five hours. An alliance that occupies it for 2 hours 30 minutes straight wins on the spot. If no alliance occupies it for 2 hours 30 minutes straight, the alliance with the longest total occupation time at the end of the five hours wins.

The difference in that total was six seconds.
When it was over, no one could say a word.
I never want to lose like that again,
and I never want the people who showed up to go through it either.
So the day after that loss,
I started building the tool.

For those five hours, I had three game accounts open at once, watching for enemy rallies. When one appeared, I judged within 5–10 seconds whether it was a single attack or a chain, and posted the landing time in chat as four digits.

Everyone who got it did the subtraction on their own.

Landing time−Your march time=Your departure time

March times differ for each person, so everyone had to come up with a different answer. Players heading to the base solo had to press at that exact moment, on a screen that doesn't show the time. After giving the call, all I could do was pray.

I built that math into the tool. Press the call once, and the landing time appears. Rally leaders and solo players alike get a countdown to the moment to press.

I thought: if I share what I'm thinking while I command, won't our win rate go up?

I don't want commanding to be a special ability that only one person has. Where to look, what to judge by, how to communicate: if everyone shares that, there are always parts others can take on. I wanted to end the state where all of it lives only inside one person's head.

I built it because I wanted to line up every landing time I could think of, but also so that the commander doesn't have to carry it all alone.

FAQ

Questions before you sign up.

Can I use it on my own?

Yes. When the caller presses the call button on the player screen, the landing time appears and “[Alliance] Rally 1234” is copied. Even if members aren't in the tool, pasting that line into in-game chat works as the call.

Is this cheating, or against the game's rules?

CommandClock never touches the game. It doesn't read the game screen, and it doesn't press anything automatically. It only shows a countdown on a separate screen. You press the button yourself.

Does every member need to install an app?

No. Just open the URL in a browser and enter the Room ID and join code. No installation needed.

Do I have to pay for members, too?

No. When one organizer signs up, everyone in the alliance or server can join at no extra cost. We don't count people or devices.

Can I set the landing time myself?

Yes. Besides calls for right now, there are calls with a landing time you set.

What if device clocks are off?

No problem. Countdowns are based on the server's time, not each device's clock. Network delay is corrected automatically too, so even on a device whose clock is a few seconds off, everyone's countdown hits 0 at the same moment.

Do I have to enter my march time every time?

No. Enter it once, and it's saved on your device. Re-enter it only when your distance changes.

Can I cancel anytime?

Yes. You can cancel yourself anytime from the cancellation page. Cancel during the free trial, and you pay nothing. If you cancel during a paid period, you won't be billed again, and you can keep using it until the end of that period. There's no minimum contract.

Is my card information safe?

Payments are processed by Stripe. We never receive or store your card information.

Can I use the console on a phone?

Yes. But it's a screen that puts three alliances side by side so you can compare them while giving calls. For readability, speed, and ease of tapping, we recommend a PC. If you're commanding, a PC is best. As a guide: Windows 10/11 or macOS, an Intel Core i3 or AMD Ryzen 3 or better, 8 GB of memory or more, and a screen at least 1366 pixels wide. A PC bought within the last five years or so should be fine. The player screen that members use is designed for phones.

Is it only for one specific game?

No. It works in any game where several people need to act at the same time by the clock.

What if it doesn't work properly?

If the service can't be provided properly, we'll handle it case by case. Contact details are in the Legal notice.
