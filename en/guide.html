1. What this tool does

In a word, it’s a tool that “tells each teammate their own moment to press, with a single call.”

Say four players want to land on the same base at the same time. But their cities are in different places on the map, so their march times to the base differ. One takes 3 minutes; another arrives in 45 seconds.

If they all press together on “3, 2, 1,” the closest players land first. To land at the same time, you need to “press earlier the farther you are, and later the closer you are.” The moment to press is different for each person.

To line up everyone’s landing at 21:03:15

Player A  march 3:00  →  press at 21:00:15
Player B  march 2:15  →  press at 21:01:00
Player C  march 1:30  →  press at 21:01:45
Player D  march 0:45  →  press at 21:02:30
                     ↓
       Everyone lands at 21:03:15

Doing this subtraction for every player, to the second, over and over during a live battle is too much for a person. With CommandClock, the organizer just presses the call once at the moment they want, and the tool calculates the landing time. The tool also builds this table and sends each device a countdown just for that player.

You don’t need to worry about clock drift

When you’re lining things up to the second, a phone clock that’s a few seconds off ruins everything. CommandClock uses the server’s time as its reference instead of each device’s clock, and also subtracts the round-trip network time to correct for it. Users never need to reset their clocks.

2. The three roles

Everyone who joins first chooses their role. There are only three.

Officer — the organizer

The person who gives the call when it’s time to rally. One per group is enough. The officer’s screen lists every member’s departure time.

Rally leader — starts rallies

The player who sends out their own rally toward the target. Their screen shows “Depart in ○ seconds,” a countdown just for them. March times differ by player, so they enter their own march time once at the start.

Up to 6 can register per group (squad).

Joiner — joins rallies

A player who doesn’t start rallies but joins a rally leader’s rally. There’s no limit on the number, and no extra charge no matter how many join.

3. From signing up to getting started

Sign up

In the pricing section, choose a plan and pay by card. When payment is done, the completion screen shows three things: the URL, Room ID, and join code. The same details are also sent to your registered email address.

The Room ID and join code are your group’s own key. Other customers can’t see them.

Share the three things with members

Send the URL, Room ID, and join code to your members by chat or similar. No app installation needed; they can use it by opening it in their phone’s browser.

*The built-in browser inside social media apps may not work properly. Choosing “Open in browser” is the safe option.

Each person chooses a role and registers

Opening the URL shows a screen for entering the Room ID and join code. After joining, each person chooses their role from “Officer,” “Rally leader,” or “Joiner” and enters a name. Rally leaders also enter their own march time.

What you enter is saved on your device, so next time you just choose.

The officer gives the call

On the officer’s screen, press the call button when it’s time to rally. At that moment, everyone’s screen starts moving on its own timing. Then each person just moves the moment their screen says “Start.”

4. What to do on each screen

Seeing it move is faster than reading, so take a look first. The 30 seconds from giving a call until everyone lands are in the demo on the top page.

Participant screen (used by everyone)

  • Shows a large clock and your own countdown.
  • All you do is “move the moment the countdown hits 0.”
  • It’s also read aloud, so you’ll know even if you’re not looking at the screen.

Officer screen (used by the organizer)

  • Lists, for everyone in your group, the time until they press, their rally time, their march time, and their landing time.
  • Press the call when it’s time to rally. The tool calculates the landing time. If you want to set a time yourself, you can also enter the landing time directly.
  • The “Copy” button turns everyone’s departure times into text. Paste it straight into chat, and even members who don’t have the tool open can get it.

Example of the copied text

[Rally time] ○○ Alliance
Squad 1  Landing 21:03:15
  Player A  Depart 21:00:15 (March 3:00)
  Player B  Depart 21:01:00 (March 2:15)

Useful even when members can’t open the tool. Available on every plan.

Console / Commander console (Commander Plan only)

  • See three alliances together on one screen.
  • Give calls across multiple alliances at the same time (paired calls).
  • Swap calls: a call to hand Sunfire Castle, taken from the enemy state by the attacking alliance, to the occupying alliance. You can send, all at once, the landing time for the occupying alliance’s solo marches and how many seconds before that landing time to leave the garrison (withdraw).
  • Withdraw: each player in the garrison leaves at the same moment, following the countdown on their own screen. If the flag holder, alliance leader, or officers pull people out one by one, you won’t make it in a one-second race.
  • Add /admin to the end of the URL, open it, and join with the same Room ID and join code.

We recommend using the console on a PC

It also works on a phone. But the console puts three alliances side by side on one screen so you can compare them while giving calls. For readability, speed, and ease of tapping, a PC is best. On a phone, the alliances stack vertically, so you’ll be scrolling up and down while you operate it.

A guide to the minimum you’ll need.

  • PC: Windows 10/11 or macOS
  • Browser: the latest Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Safari
  • CPU: Intel Core i3 or AMD Ryzen 3 or better (a PC bought within the last five years or so should be fine)
  • Memory: 8 GB or more
  • Screen: at least 1366 pixels wide (a typical laptop screen). With Full HD (1920×1080), you can view the three alliances side by side with room to spare
  • Connection: a stable internet connection (wired, or Wi-Fi with a steady signal)

The player screen that members use is designed for phones.

Reinforce calls are on the officer’s screen, too

When the enemy attacks you in a chain, slipping your own troops in between to defend is reinforcing. Set how many seconds before the last landing to send them in, and a signal worked backward from that time is sent out. This is an officer feature, so it’s available on every plan。

6. When things don’t work

I entered the Room ID and join code but can’t get in

Check that there are no spaces before or after them. Codes are case-sensitive. If you still can’t get in, try copying the details from the email we sent exactly as they are.

It says “up to 6 devices at once” and won’t let me in

Personal plans purchased before September 24, 2026 allow up to 6 devices open at once, joiners included. Close devices or tabs you aren’t using and try again. To remove the limit, please sign up for the Alliance Plan.

It says “This squad is full”

Up to 6 rally leaders can register per squad. Choose another squad, or ask your officer about open slots. Joiners have no limit, so they can join as they are.

Only my countdown doesn’t move

You may not have finished registering. Check that you entered your name and march time and pressed “Register and start.” If your name appears in the list on the officer’s screen, you’re registered.

I paid, but my Room ID hasn’t arrived

Please check your spam folder. If you can’t find it, contact support@commandclock.jp . As soon as we confirm the payment, we’ll issue it and send it to you.

I want to cancel

The cancellation page lets you cancel yourself anytime. Enter the email address you signed up with, and Stripe will send you a confirmation code; enter it to cancel.
